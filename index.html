Why now

Agent sprawl is outrunning oversight

Copilots, RPA-plus-agentic workflows, and custom LangGraph/AutoGen builds have moved from pilots into production across underwriting, claims, procurement, HR screening and pricing — often stood up by individual business units, outside the visibility of risk, compliance or even IT.

Regulation has caught up faster than governance has: the EU AI Act's Annex III high-risk obligations are now binding for systems used in credit, insurance, employment and essential services; ISO/IEC 42001 gives boards a certifiable AI management standard to be measured against; and the NIST AI RMF is fast becoming the reference model auditors ask for by name.

Most organizations can answer “which AI models do we license.” Very few can answer “which of our agents can take an irreversible action on a customer's behalf without a human in the loop” — and that is the question a regulator, a board, or a plaintiff's lawyer will ask first.

What we measure

Five instruments, checked every cycle

Each Critical- or High-risk agent is scored against fixed thresholds every reporting period. A single failed check becomes a named exception with recommended remediation in the Assurance Report — nothing is graded on a curve.

Hallucination rate
≤ 5.0%
Automated groundedness / entailment scoring against source data, backed by scheduled human-reviewed sampling in production.
Guardrail bypass count
≤ 1 / quarter
Scheduled automated red-teaming attempts to jailbreak or bypass each agent's safety layer; every successful bypass is logged as an exception.
Task success rate
≥ 90.0%
A golden-set benchmark of representative scenarios, run on a schedule and scored against expected outcomes, plus live escalation and override rates.
Behavioral drift score
≤ 0.15
Current-period behavior compared statistically against an established baseline, to catch silent model or configuration changes before they cause harm.
Bias / disparate-impact screen
Pass / fail
Counterfactual prompt pairs and outcome-parity analysis run across protected attributes to catch systematic disparities in agent decisions.

Before you ask

The questions every client asks first

Do you need access to our model weights or training data?+

No. Both tiers sit outside the model, observing inputs, outputs, tool calls and logs. Nothing about your proprietary models or training data has to leave your environment.

How is this different from the MLOps monitoring we already run?+

MLOps monitoring watches infrastructure health — latency, uptime, classic model drift. This is a governance and assurance layer on top of it: mapping every agent to a regulatory framework, testing for hallucination, guardrail bypass and bias, and producing a report a board or regulator can rely on.

Is our data used to train or improve any models?+

No. Observability data captured during the engagement is used only to score and report on your agents — never to train models for us or for any other client.

What if we don't want the managed service after the diagnostic?+

You keep the risk register, the classification of every agent, and a prioritized remediation roadmap either way. Tier 2 is an option, not a lock-in.
